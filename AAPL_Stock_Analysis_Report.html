1. Data Overview

The dataset contains daily Open, High, Low, Close, Volume and Adjusted Close prices for AAPL. Over the period, the closing price ranged from $90.34 to $135.51, delivering a total return of 5.9%.

2. Price Trend & Moving Averages

The 10-day moving average tracks short-term momentum while the 30-day MA smooths out noise to reveal the broader trend. Crossovers between the two are a classic (if noisy) signal traders watch for.

3. Daily Returns Distribution

Daily returns cluster tightly around zero and are roughly bell-shaped, though with fatter tails than a true normal distribution — occasional large moves (earnings releases, market shocks) are more common than a Gaussian model would predict.

4. Trading Volume

Volume spikes typically coincide with earnings announcements or major news events, and often precede periods of higher volatility.

5. Feature Correlations

Open/High/Low/Close are (unsurprisingly) almost perfectly correlated with each other. Volume and volatility show weaker, more independent relationships with price — these are the more informative features for modeling.

6. Predictive Modeling: Next-Day Closing Price

Three approaches were compared, trained on the first 80% of the (time-ordered) data and evaluated on the final 20%:

Key finding: the naive baseline — simply predicting tomorrow's close equals today's close — performed as well as or better than both the Linear Regression and Random Forest models. This is a well-known and important result in finance: daily stock prices closely follow a random walk, so short-horizon price levels are extremely hard to out-predict with standard features. High R² here is largely explained by day-to-day prices being naturally close to each other, not by the model capturing real signal.

The best model's predictions track the actual price closely — but so would simply carrying forward yesterday's price, which is the honest baseline to beat.

8. Conclusions

  • AAPL delivered a total return of 5.9% over the sample period, with average daily volatility of 1.53%.
  • Price and volume data show clear trend and event-driven volatility patterns visible in moving averages and volume spikes.
  • Neither Linear Regression nor Random Forest meaningfully beat a naive "no-change" forecast for next-day closing price — consistent with the efficient-market / random-walk hypothesis at a one-day horizon.
  • Practical takeaway: point-forecasting next-day price is not where ML adds value here. More promising directions would be predicting direction (up/down) with a classifier, modeling volatility (e.g. GARCH), using longer lookback windows and technical indicators (RSI, MACD), or forecasting over longer horizons where fundamentals matter more than daily noise.
